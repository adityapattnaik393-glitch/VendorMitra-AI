# Practical pricing for fruit and vegetable vendors

This guide is a planning checklist, not a live market-price feed or a promise
of profit. Produce prices, quality, waste, and local demand vary by market and
day.

## Estimate a selling price

For a batch, first estimate its total cost:

- Purchase cost paid to the supplier.
- Transport and market fees paid for that batch.
- Packaging or other per-batch costs.
- An allowance for damaged or unsold produce, based on the vendor's own
  records.

Divide the batch cost by the quantity expected to be sold, not automatically
by the quantity purchased. This gives an approximate break-even cost per unit.
The selling price also needs to account for the vendor's intended earnings and
what customers in that market are willing to pay. These are vendor decisions,
not universal fixed percentages.

## Daily routine

1. Record each purchase, transport cost, quantity, and date.
2. Note the quantity sold and remaining at the end of the day.
3. Compare the observed local prices for similar quality and quantity.
4. Set and display a clear price per kilogram, piece, or bundle.
5. Review the estimate when supplier prices, season, quality, or waste changes.
6. Avoid misleading weights, hidden charges, or claims about quality that
   cannot be supported.

## Reduce waste without misleading customers

- Buy quantities that match the stall's recent sales records.
- Keep produce clean, shaded, and handled appropriately for that product.
- Separate damaged items and describe their condition honestly.
- Consider a clearly labeled end-of-day offer only when the vendor chooses it
  and the displayed price and quantity are accurate.

Do not treat this guide as current local price information. Check today's
supplier and market prices directly and follow applicable local rules.
